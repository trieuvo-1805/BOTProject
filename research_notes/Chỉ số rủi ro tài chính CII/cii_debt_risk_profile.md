# CII (HOSE: CII): debt structure, bond obligations and qualitative financial risk profile (consolidated)

Research date: 15/11/2026. All sources are Vietnamese financial press (Báo Pháp Luật/Doanh Nhân, CafeF, Viettimes, DNSE, Thời báo Tài chính, Stockbiz, Mekong ASEAN). I did not reach primary HNX (cbonds.hnx.vn) or CII IR documents directly, so bond details come from press reports of CII disclosures. Figures can differ between outlets and between parent-only and consolidated statements, and those differences are flagged below.

## 1. Outstanding bonds, terms, maturity wall, extensions and restructuring

### Takeaway
CII has moved its bond funding toward very long-dated, unsecured convertible bonds sold to retail investors and shareholders (10–25-year terms, conversion prices of 10,000–12,500 VND). It tops this up with small, secured 2–5-year private bonds, which it repurchases early. These convertibles reduce near-term cash principal risk. In exchange they create heavy dilution and placement risk, plus a 14.5% coupon step-up on CII426001 from about 2029/2030. Evidence of strain in 2025–2026: proceeds were disbursed late, an early-repayment plan (CII012029_G) was delayed by bondholder negotiations, and the CII426001 rights issue had to be extended because unsold rights remained, including those of the state shareholder HFIC.

### Inferences
- **Maturity wall 2026–2030:** cash-settled straight bonds look modest and are being repurchased early: CII12402/12403/12404 (about 408 billion VND, due 2027), CIIL12601 (200 billion VND, due 2028), CII12503 (280 billion VND, due 2030) and CII012029_G (due 2029, possibly already redeemed). The main bond risk is not a principal wall. It is (a) placement and refinancing risk on recurring large convertible issues, (b) dependence on CII's share price staying above 10,000–12,500 VND so that holders convert, (c) the CII426001 coupon jumping to 14.5% from about 2029/2030, and (d) dilution: charter capital is about 6,720 billion VND, and CII426001 alone could add about 672 million shares, roughly doubling the share count.
- **Lack of transparency:** convertible proceeds labelled "debt restructuring" sat partly undisbursed, and timelines were extended. This is a governance and transparency risk signal.
- The secured private bonds are pledged against shares of group companies (CEE, CII PPP). This creates a cross-collateral chain: if CEE's share price falls, collateral is weakened.
- No payment default, coupon delay or formal bond restructuring was found for 2022–2026. The only extensions found were to offering periods and use-of-proceeds deadlines.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full HNX bond list (cbonds.hnx.vn), so the complete outstanding bond list and per-issue balances at 11/2026 were not verified. The CII42013 convertible (2020) and other older issues were not checked.
- The final placement result of CII426001 (after 03/11/2026), the CII425002 result and coupon, and whether CII012029_G was redeemed in Q2/2026 were not found.
- The CII425001 coupon formula (the fixed and floating components) was not found.
- The outcome of the 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting (record date 05/11/2026; agenda: 2027–2032 infrastructure direction and capital raising) was not found.

## 2. Bank loan exposure, refinancing, interest rates, lenders

### Takeaway
Consolidated borrowings were about 21.7 trillion VND at 31/03/2026, rising to about 24.5 trillion VND (loans) or about 27.8 trillion VND including convertible bonds at 30/06/2026. That is about 1.9–2.1x equity, roughly 75% long-term. Interest expense of about 1.28 trillion VND in 2025 and about 706 billion VND in H1/2026 absorbs most operating profit, and operating cash flow turned negative in 2025 and Q1/2026. A new project loan of up to 27.1 trillion VND for the HCMC–Trung Lương–Mỹ Thuận expansion BOT will sit at the project company level.

### Inferences
- **Interest coverage:** H1/2026 pre-tax profit was about 160 billion VND against about 706 billion VND of interest. EBIT/interest is therefore only about 1.2x, and well below 1x if gains on investment property sales are excluded.
- If the CII group consolidates the 55%-owned Saigon–Mỹ Thuận BOT company, consolidated debt could rise by up to about 27 trillion VND over 2026–2028. That would roughly double borrowings, with no toll cash flow until 2029. Construction cost and delay risk become consolidated leverage risk.
- Vietnamese bank project loans are typically floating-rate. Combined with floating-rate bond tranches, most of the debt stack is likely exposed to rising rates, but this was not confirmed (see Gaps).

### Gaps

- No lender for the 27.1 trillion VND Trung Lương–Mỹ Thuận loan has been confirmed.

## 3. Group structure, consolidated leverage, and reliance on one-off gains

### Takeaway
CII is a holding company whose consolidated debt sits largely in BOT subsidiaries (through LGC/CII Bridge & Road) and real estate (NBB, Khu Bắc Thủ Thiêm). Reported profit has repeatedly depended on non-recurring items: divestment gains (2022), remeasurement of NBB (2024), investment property sales (H1/2026), and possibly unrealized PC1 gains. Core profit after interest is thin.

## 4. BOT-specific risks, BT/land payments, and receivables from the state

### Takeaway
Toll revenue (about 1.4 trillion VND in H1/2026, more than 85% of gross revenue) is CII's core cash engine. Accounting has shifted so that BOT "benefits" are now recovered through toll revenue instead of booked as financial income, which lowers reported profit. The Thủ Thiêm BT settlement, paid in land valued at 2015/2016 prices under Decree 91/2025, has moved forward after years of delay. Converting that land into cash still depends on HCMC handovers and real estate sales.

### Inferences
- **Key BOT risk indicators:** toll revenue growth by station, the share of toll revenue in revenue, toll-rights amortization, and policy risk (toll fee adjustments, station relocation, contract renegotiation with the Ministry of Construction or HCMC).
- **Pipeline risk:** the pipeline (more than 100 trillion VND) is roughly 2.5x current total assets. It implies large future capital calls and equity-raising needs. This is the main driver of continuous convertible issuance.
- **BT land risk:** the BT land payment turns receivables and construction spending into real estate inventory. Risk shifts to land handover timing, land price determination and the Thủ Thiêm property market.

- The Thủ Thiêm–Long Thành and Rạch Chiếc bridge BOT/BT payment status was not found.
- The total BT receivable balance and the 9-lot handover schedule were not quantified.

## 5. Credit ratings, analyst commentary, liquidity crunch periods, and 2025–2026 developments

### Takeaway
No public credit rating for CII by FiinRatings, S&I Ratings, VIS Rating or Saigon Ratings was found. Press commentary consistently frames CII as highly leveraged ("loay hoay với bài toán nợ và dòng tiền" = struggling with debt and cash flow) and reliant on capital-market refinancing. The clearest stress signals in 2025–2026 are negative operating cash flow, delayed use of bond proceeds, and a rights issue that failed to attract buyers for the state shareholder's rights.

### Inferences
- **Suggested CII-specific risk indicators:**
  1. Interest coverage (EBIT, and EBITDA excluding one-off gains, divided by interest).
  2. Operating cash flow after interest.
  3. Net debt / equity, and net debt / toll EBITDA.
  4. Short-term debt and bonds due within 12 months versus cash.
  5. Share of profit from non-recurring items (divestments, remeasurements, investment property sales, PC1 fair-value gains).
  6. Convertible bond balance, CII share price versus conversion prices (10,000 / 12,500 VND), and dilution ratio.
  7. Placement success rate and offering extensions on new issues.
  8. Undisbursed proceeds and use-of-proceeds delays.
  9. Toll revenue growth by project.
  10. Pledged group shares (CEE, LGC) and their market value.
  11. BT/Thủ Thiêm land handover milestones.
  12. Debt of off-balance-sheet or about-to-be-consolidated project companies (Saigon–Mỹ Thuận BOT, up to 27.1 trillion VND).
  13. Coupon step-up exposure (CII426001 at 14.5% after year 3).
- No payment default was found in 2022–2023. CII appears to have handled that crisis through early buybacks and convertible issuance. This is not firmly verified.
